# Attention Is All You Need
**Authors:** Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones, Aidan N. Gomez, Lukasz Kaiser, Illia Polosukhin
**arXiv:** 1706.03762 | **Published:** 2017-06-12 | http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762v7

## Why it matters
The paper shows that removing recurrence and convolution in favor of pure attention yields a model that trains faster, scales better, and achieves new state‑of‑the‑art translation quality, demonstrating that attention alone can capture global dependencies and reshape sequence‑to‑sequence modeling.

## Problem
Sequential computation in recurrent and convolutional encoder‑decoder models limits parallelism and makes training on long sequences slow and memory‑intensive.

## Method
- Introduce the Transformer architecture that replaces recurrent and convolutional layers with multi‑headed self‑attention.
- Use positional encodings (sinusoidal) to inject order information.
- Employ an encoder‑decoder structure where both encoder and decoder consist of stacked self‑attention and feed‑forward sub‑layers.
- Apply dropout, layer normalization, and residual connections for regularization and stable training.
- Train models on GPUs using beam search with checkpoint averaging for inference.

## Key results
- BLEU 28.4 on WMT 2014 English‑to‑German translation (big model).
- BLEU 41.8 on WMT 2014 English‑to‑French translation (single model).
- Training time 3.5 days on 8 P100 GPUs for the big model.
- Training time as little as twelve hours on eight P100 GPUs for the base model.
- Big model uses dropout 0.1 (instead of 0.3) and achieves BLEU 41.0 on English‑to‑French.
- Base model achieved BLEU 25.8 on English‑to‑German development set with 65 M parameters.

## Limitations
- (inferred) The quadratic memory and compute cost of self‑attention may limit scalability to very long sequences.
- (inferred) The approach is evaluated only on machine translation and parsing, so generalization to other modalities is unproven.
- (inferred) Training still requires multiple high‑end GPUs, which may be prohibitive for some researchers.
- (inferred) The model relies on large amounts of parallel data; performance on low‑resource languages is not reported.

## Suggested follow-up questions
- How does the Transformer perform on tasks with much longer input sequences than those in translation?
- Can the attention‑only architecture be adapted efficiently for non‑text modalities such as images or audio?
- What are the trade‑offs between model size (base vs. big) and inference latency in real‑time applications?
- How does the lack of recurrence affect the model’s ability to capture hierarchical linguistic structures?
- Are there effective ways to reduce the quadratic complexity of self‑attention without sacrificing accuracy?
